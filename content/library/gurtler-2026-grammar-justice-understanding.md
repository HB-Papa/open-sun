Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
Current energy transitions are characterized by contradictory, often irreconcilable demands for justice and the question of which justice concerns should take priority. Pragmatic approaches to meaningfully engage with diverse justice claims are needed in both policy and research. The aim of this contribution, therefore, is to clarify key questions researchers and practitioners must consider to understand contradictory justice claims. While recognizing that resolving such conflicts ultimately remains a political process, we argue that conceptual choices and normative assumptions should be clarified to identify and deal with justice contradictions. For practitioners, a systematic approach to addressing a multitude of justice claims can enable more substantiated and transparent decision-making. This contribution sheds light on the normative uncertainty hindering many processes of energy transitions and attempts to systematize the discussion in the energy justice literature by working towards a “grammar of justice”. We draw on the three-tenets framework of energy justice (recognition, distribution, participation) and develop a framework to contextualize diverging justice claims, focusing on claim holders (subjects),claim addressees, scale, time frame, objects, grounds of justice, informational base, principles, instruments, and body of knowledge. We provide a set of questions to operationalize this framework, helping to identify and address justice conflicts pragmatically. The article, thereby, offers a foundational structure for advancing a shared understanding of justice in the political shaping of energy transitions.
