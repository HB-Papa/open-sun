An energy shortfall affects around 200 million people in West Africa. Reviewing the relevant laws, the authors find that older investment treaties between pairs of countries favor fossil fuel projects and let investors sue governments in international arbitration, which clashes with climate goals and fairness. The study recommends a shared regional investment framework under the African Continental Free Trade Area, with climate exemptions in treaties, rules requiring investors to use local workers and suppliers, and support for struggling utilities.

**Why it matters:** Shows how old investment rules can lock in fossil fuels, and how fairer ones could let West African countries build clean energy that benefits local people.
