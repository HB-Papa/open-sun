Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
Renewable energy is central to climate mitigation and to SDG 7, yet its wider contribution to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) is not automatic. Existing studies identify potential synergies with health, employment, infrastructure, poverty reduction, and climate action, alongside trade-offs involving land, water, food systems, biodiversity, materials, waste, and the distribution of costs and benefits. Because recent global syntheses have already mapped renewable-energy contributions across all 17 SDGs, a further general mapping would offer limited novelty. This paper therefore develops a complementary conceptual framework through an integrative synthesis of research on SDG interactions, renewable-energy life cycles, technology-specific effects, energy access, environmental justice, and impact evaluation. The framework links context and governance to deployment attributes, life-cycle activities, mediating mechanisms, target-level SDG outcomes, distributional outcomes, and feedback over time, and it introduces a five-level evidence ladder that distinguishes stated alignment and potential interactions from outputs, observed outcomes, and attributable impacts. The synthesis indicates that service quality and actual use condition the development effects of access, that technology and supply-chain choices shape environmental burdens, and that participation and distributional arrangements determine who benefits and who bears costs. Six testable propositions, an operational indicator scheme, and a research agenda are proposed. The paper is a transparent conceptual synthesis based on a discovery-led literature search rather than an exhaustive systematic review, and it does not claim that any single renewable technology uniformly advances the SDGs.
