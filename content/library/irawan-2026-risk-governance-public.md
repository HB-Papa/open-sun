Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
The development of nuclear power in Indonesia raises questions about how regulatory oversight and public acceptance are addressed within energy policy. This article examines the relationship between nuclear risk governance and public acceptance through qualitative document analysis and a narrative synthesis of scientific literature, regulatory documents, institutional reports, and secondary survey data. The analysis identifies established procedures for technical risk assessment and ongoing developments in radioactive waste regulation, alongside public concerns regarding environmental impacts, coastal livelihoods, and participation in decision-making. Differences between provincial survey findings and documented local opposition suggest that general support for nuclear energy should not be equated with acceptance of development at a particular location. Drawing on risk governance and energy justice, the discussion distinguishes regulatory compliance from the institutional consideration of affected communities. The study contributes to understanding how technical oversight, public participation, and institutional accountability intersect in Indonesia's nuclear power policy, while recognizing the limitations of documentary evidence in assessing their practical effectiveness.
