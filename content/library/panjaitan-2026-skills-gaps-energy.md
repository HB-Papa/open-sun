Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
Indonesia’s electricity plan for 2025–2034 projects 69.5 GW of additional generation capacity, including 17.1 GW of solar power, while government estimates associate the plan with 348,057 solar-related job opportunities. These projections make workforce capability a material implementation issue: renewable infrastructure requires technicians, engineers, safety specialists, project managers and locally available operations-and-maintenance personnel. Yet headline job estimates do not reveal whether workers are available in the required occupations, locations or project phases. This article examines how workforce development can be linked to the resilience of solar infrastructure in an archipelagic state. It uses a focused qualitative documentary analysis, official Indonesian planning and employment documents, and international institutional evidence, interpreted through energy security, capacity development and energy justice perspectives. The analysis identifies four interlocking governance gaps: incomplete skills intelligence; weak alignment between training, certification and project demand; uneven territorial distribution of operational capability; and limited feedback from infrastructure performance into workforce planning. The article proposes a territorialized solar workforce resilience framework in which workforce data, competency standards, work-based learning, local maintenance capacity and critical-infrastructure performance are planned together. The framework is a theory-building contribution, not an estimate of causal effects. Before journal submission, its propositions require validation through comparative project-level fieldwork and verified occupational data.
