Williams reads solarpunk as an “energy imaginary”: a shared picture of what life powered by the sun could look like. He asks what the genre reveals about the look and feel of solar technology, how its imagined infrastructures bundle together the social and technical pieces a transition needs, and what it suggests about community-building and agency in today’s struggles over energy.

**Why it matters:** A foundational academic reading of solarpunk that connects the genre’s aesthetics to real debates about energy transition.
