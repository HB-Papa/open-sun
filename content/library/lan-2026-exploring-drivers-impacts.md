Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
The construction sector is a major contributor to the climate crisis. In China, rapid urbanisation has intensified these effects, prompting cities like Guangzhou to implement construction halt measures targeting high-emission and resource-intensive projects. While these measures aim to reduce pollution and carbon emissions, their systemic consequences remain poorly understood. This paper addresses this gap by investigating the environmental, social, and economic impacts of construction halts in Guangzhou through a systems thinking approach. We integrated desk-based analysis of halted construction projects, expert interviews, and a public survey in a causal loop diagram, displaying hypotheses of the causal chains underpinning construction halt measures. Findings suggest that construction halts can mitigate soil, water, air, and noise pollution, and reduce direct and indirect carbon emissions. However, they may also give rise to unintended effects, including unemployment, debt risks, and public opposition, particularly when applied through a “one-size-fits-all” governance model. As moratoria on new construction gain traction globally, our results highlight the limitations of authoritarian environmentalism, and underscore the need for context-sensitive, deliberative, and participatory governance to implement a new post-growth planning paradigm to achieve ecological justice and urban well-being.
