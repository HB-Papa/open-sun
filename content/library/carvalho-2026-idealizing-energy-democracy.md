The authors study two disputes in Portugal, over lithium mining in the north and a planned large solar plant in the Alentejo. Both projects, they find, favor corporate ownership and sideline local people, creating “green sacrifice zones,” places harmed for clean energy. The authors write a fictional scenario set in a Central Portuguese ecovillage in 2035, where residents run their own energy transition. The authors suggest fiction might help people imagine energy democracy, in which communities share control of their energy.

**Why it matters:** Shows fiction being used as a research tool to picture community-run clean energy instead of top-down megaprojects, much as solarpunk stories do.
