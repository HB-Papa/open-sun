Abstract from DOAJ (for reference; not shown on the site):
Abstract Objectives This study examines the association between electric vehicle (EV) adoption and life-cycle energy inequality during the global energy transition. It addresses the limited attention given to distributional outcomes beyond the environmental benefits of EV deployment. Theoretical framework The analysis integrates Energy Justice Theory and Global Value Chain Theory to explain how infrastructure, economic opportunities, environmental burdens, and value creation are distributed unevenly across countries during technological transitions. Method A two-stage empirical design is employed. First, a Life-Cycle Energy Inequality Index (LCEII) is constructed using principal component analysis based on data from 53 countries. Second, a balanced panel of 13 emerging and developing economies covering 2010–2024 is analysed using fixed-effects estimation, with dynamic instrumental-variable specifications employed as robustness checks. Results and discussion EV adoption is positively and significantly associated with life-cycle energy inequality, indicating that technological diffusion may intensify distributional imbalances. Recycling capacity and domestic value-added participation are negatively associated with inequality, whereas grid carbon intensity is positively associated with it. These findings indicate that structural capabilities and associated governance mechanisms shape the distributional outcomes of the EV transition. Implications EV-adoption policies should be adapted to national income, market maturity, recycling capability, and clean-energy infrastructure. EV diffusion should therefore be accompanied by circular-economy development, domestic industrial upgrading, and equitable infrastructure investment. Originality/value This study introduces the LCEII as a multidimensional measure of the distributional outcomes of the EV transition. By integrating Energy Justice and Global Value Chain perspectives within a cross-country framework, it provides new evidence on the structural conditions required for a more equitable energy transition.
