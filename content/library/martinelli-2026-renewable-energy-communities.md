Abstract from DOAJ (for reference; not shown on the site):
The institutionalisation of Renewable Energy Communities (RECs) within the European Union regulatory framework has proceeded in parallel with a rapid expansion of the scientific literature on community-based energy systems, yet the relationship between these two trajectories has not been systematically examined. This paper addresses this gap by combining two comparative analyses: a structured review of EU energy directives, from RED I (2001/77/EC) to RED III (2023/2413), and a bibliometric analysis of 2,684 Scopus-indexed European publications (2001-2025), conducted according to the PRISMA protocol and processed through VOSviewer, for keyword co-occurrence mapping, and Biblioshiny, for diachronic trend analysis. The corpus is divided into two sub-periods: before and after the adoption of RED II (2018) to isolate the effects of the formal recognition of RECs on the structure of the research field. Results show exponential growth in scientific output after 2018, with an average annual growth rate of 28.93% and a peak of 445 publications in 2025, accompanied by a structural thematic shift from technology-centred clusters to an integrated discourse organised around governance, spatial planning, prosumer participation and energy justice. The pre-2018 literature had already produced the conceptual foundations subsequently codified in EU legislation, suggesting a pattern of mutual alignment between scientific agendas and regulatory priorities rather than a simple linear, top-down influence. These findings have direct implications for urban spatial planning, suggesting that integrating RECs into municipal governance tools, such as Sustainable Energy and Climate Action Plans (SECAPs) and municipal master plans, requires multi-scalar regulatory frameworks that explicitly allocate space, competences, and participation mechanisms at the neighbourhood level. By linking regulatory milestones and scientific trajectories, the paper shows how EU energy policy and REC-related research have evolved in close temporal and thematic alignment, reshaping the vocabulary, priorities and instruments of urban energy governance and highlighting concrete opportunities for technicians, planners and policymakers to use RECs as levers for more just and spatially coherent energy transitions in European cities.
