Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
The energy transition is routinely framed as a technical challenge. But transitions are socio-political undertakings. The struggle over what counts as a “just transition” is a struggle over what a liveable future should look like. Facts alone cannot settle this dilemma because the struggle rests on competing ideas about what a just transition is for . Detailed economic and physical scenarios map climate trajectories, but no comparable map exists of the internal rationales behind the transition narratives. Social science should fill that gap. This paper analyses public statements on the transition made in Australia between 2022 and 2025 by industry, government, think-tanks, civil-society and First Nations organisations. Using structuralist semiotic analysis, which reads each position as a structure of meaning built around a central opposition, we first construct five ideal-typical justice scenarios: Defensive Localism, Instrumental Accelerationism, Progressive Extractivism, Reformist Transition and Transformative Transition. The analysis reveals that each scenario has a structural blind spot: something it must ignore to remain internally consistent. We then draw an “Overton-Venn diagram” plotting the scenarios against two anchors that together form the contradiction all scenarios must manage: economic growth and biophysical planetary boundaries. The diagram shows that three of the scenarios treat growth and planetary boundaries as reconcilable, whereas Defensive Localism denies planetary boundaries and Transformative Transition challenges growth. Our aim is to articulate what each scenario assumes, where they might overlap and where each meets material limits, so that, within the politically and materially possible, contestation can move from antagonism toward productive disagreement.
