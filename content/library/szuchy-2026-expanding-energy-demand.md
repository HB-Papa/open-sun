Electricity use by data centers, increasingly driven by artificial intelligence, is growing far faster than overall demand and is clustered in a few places. Combining a review of EU law with published data from Ireland, the United Kingdom and Spain since 2023, the authors link it to grid bottlenecks in the regions where data centers cluster, which may push up network costs. Household bills mostly followed gas prices, taxes and other factors, and the study does not prove data centers raised bills. Still, the authors warn of a possible “double burden” on households already struggling to afford energy, who may gain least from AI yet help pay for its infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** Asks who pays for AI’s growing appetite for power, a fairness question at the heart of energy justice and of any vision of shared, clean energy.
