Abstract from DOAJ (for reference; not shown on the site):
BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION (version 2022 https://babel.utc.fr/; new version 2025 https://new.babel-revolution.fr/) is a research-creation project developed by a multidisciplinary team. It explores the relationship between biodiversity on the one hand, and linguistic and cultural diversity on the other. Moving between scholarly imaginaries, academic culture, and creative writing practices, this creative practice-based research has led to the online development of both a participatory digital artwork and a customizable narrative interface designed for use in digital writing workshops. The first part of this paper presents the theoretical framework that informed the project, at the intersection of sociolinguistics, plurilingualism as a didactic approach, and environmental concerns. BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION (version 2022) emphasizes this linguistic continuum between languages as a space of diversity and invites to draw on its richness as a way of resisting reductive standardization. Indeed the piece queries the connection between the loss of flora and the disappearance of languages through a multi-voiced narrative and opens up to a wider linguistic diversity than that of its creators. We position this work as an environmental writing, defined above all by ethical and thematic criteria, in the tradition of nature writing and American ecocriticism (Buekens, 2019). Building on the threefold theoretical framework of the biocultural appraoch (Maffi, 2002), pluralistic approaches to languages (Candelier, 2008) and heterolingual literature (Suchet, 2014), BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION proposes to experiment the link between ecology and ecological linguistics through electronic literature. The second part “diversities in digital literature” analyses digital artworks addressing issues of linguistic and/or ecological diversity. Electronic literature, though often associated with globalized platforms and the homogenizing effects of digital media (Flores, 2021), also offers fertile ground for resisting linguistic and cultural uniformity. Several digital artworks exemplify this counter-movement by foregrounding plurilingualism, interconnection, and ecological awareness. Annie Abrahams’s projects, including The Reading Club (with Emmanuel Guez), Being Human, and from estranger to e-stranger: living in between languages, explore vulnerability, care, and linguistic entanglement through networked performance art, emphasizing hybridity and “staying with the trouble” (Haraway, 2016). Amira Hanafi’s CreaTures Glossary (https://glossary.languagin.gs/) invites collaborative reflection on ecological thought through shared definitions of terms such as “entanglement,” “multi-species,” and “sustainability,” highlighting posthumanist interdependence. Eugenio Tisselli’s minimalist piece Amazon (2018) translates the destruction of the Amazon rainforest into a few lines of executable code, turning trees into numbers to expose the algorithmic logics of capitalism. Alinta Krauth’s The (m)Otherhood of Meep (the bat translator) (https://www.alintakrauth.com/) employs poetic AI translation to render interspecies communication visible, bridging the human and nonhuman. Finally, Les Évaporées (Ed Snowcrash, 2022), which won at the Concours Inalco de la nouvelle plurilingue (Prix Écriture numérique), is an interactive multilingual narrative in French English, and Esperanto, situates ecological reflection within the solarpunk ethos, inviting users to reconstruct meaning through heterolingual digital traces. The creative and technical challenges posed by Les Évaporées – linguistic, thematic, and ecological – directly inspired our own research-creation project.Beyond its artistic and aesthetic dimensions, BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION was designed to address a pedagogical need: to create a digital environment fostering heterolingual writing in language education, particularly in French as a Foreign Language. The project’s initial 2022 version sought to stimulate collaborative “wreaditing” (writing–reading) practices that encourage learners to move between languages. Grounded in Arts-based research, it was progressively refined through workshops and user feedback collected from educators and researchers during events such as the IMPEC 2024 symposium. This iterative process led to the 2025 version, which integrates scenario customization and an improved user interface. The project thus evolved from a digital literary experiment into an adaptable pedagogical tool promoting pluriliteracies, a dynamic engagement with diverse linguistic, semiotic, and digital practices. By blurring the boundaries between research and creation, authorship and collaboration, BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION invites participants to become co-authors of multilingual narratives, engaging critically and creatively with the question of linguistic diversity and the utopia of a universal language.Those interested in exploring or contributing to this evolving multilingual experiment can take part in the online version of BⱯBEL RËVOLUㅏION, where every new session becomes a small collective revolution in language and digital creation.
