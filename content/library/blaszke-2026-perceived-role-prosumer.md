Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
The energy transition requires not only the development of renewable energy sources but also the active participation of local communities in energy production and consumption processes. In this context, prosumer energy is increasingly recognised as a key component of the sustainable energy transition. Despite the growing body of research on prosumerism, relatively little attention has been devoted to how its significance is perceived by local authorities, particularly in rural areas. The aim of this article is to identify how representatives of rural municipalities in Poland perceive the role of prosumer energy in the sustainable energy transition. The study employed a survey-based research design involving representatives of 518 rural municipalities. The analysis encompassed five dimensions of the energy transition: (1) the achievement of environmental and climate objectives, (2) energy justice, (3) environmental responsibility, (4) value conflicts, and (5) local communities’ energy resilience. The findings indicate that prosumer energy is perceived as an important component of the sustainable energy transition in rural areas. Respondents attribute particular significance to its contribution to environmental protection, the reduction of greenhouse gas emissions, the strengthening of energy security, and the enhancement of local communities’ resilience to energy crises. At the same time, the results reveal a clear asymmetry in perceptions of prosumer energy, with considerably greater importance attached to its environmental, energy-related and economic functions than to the social dimension of the energy transition. Respondents also recognise the risk of unequal access to the benefits of the energy transition, particularly for low-income households, older people, and residents of multi-family buildings. The article contributes to the literature on the energy transition by providing insights into the perspectives of representatives of rural municipalities and demonstrating that the social dimension of prosumer energy remains less prominent than its contribution to environmental objectives and energy security.
