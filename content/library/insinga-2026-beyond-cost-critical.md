Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
Stationary battery selection in developing and emerging economies involves trade-offs between access to capital, reliable service, lifecycle burdens, and local governance. This review examines how these dimensions affect technology choice. This structured critical review integrates selected technical, lifecycle, and energy-justice literature. Evidence is distinguished by application, geographical transferability, and commercial maturity. The literature is synthesized qualitatively; an illustrative multicriteria template specifies possible weighting scenarios without calculating technology scores or rankings. Lead–acid and lithium-ion batteries have established applications, but their relative whole-life costs depend on operating conditions, usable capacity, replacement, and financing. Evidence on lithium iron phosphate supports its consideration for stationary services without establishing universal superiority. Sodium-ion and iron-based flow batteries offer diversification opportunities with greater uncertainty in long-term field performance. Environmental comparisons depend on lifecycle boundaries and local end-of-life practices. Priorities should be defined for a specified service and jurisdiction with affected users, operators, and authorities. Upfront affordability, lifetime performance, environmental burden, and resilience/justice should be evaluated using explicit indicators, with safeguards for unacceptable health and safety risks. Equal and priority-weight scenarios are proposed only for future testing against local performance data; no optimal weights or rank reversals are demonstrated.
