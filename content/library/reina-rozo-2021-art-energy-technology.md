Reina-Rozo argues that imagining other futures for energy is essential as corporate renewable projects spread into new regions. He traces solarpunk through three lenses: its digital origins, the short-story anthologies that defined it, and the academic reflection it has prompted. He links it to climate fiction and to Indigenous and Afrofuturist science fiction, and closes by sharing a solarpunk manifesto for others to keep building.

**Why it matters:** A widely cited overview that frames solarpunk as counter-cultural hope against centralized, corporate energy.
