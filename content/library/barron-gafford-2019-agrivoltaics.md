In a dryland test site in Arizona, the researchers grew food crops in the partial shade of solar panels. Under the panels, plants showed less drought stress, some crops yielded more, and soil held more water. The crops also cooled the panels, which made them work more efficiently.

**Why it matters:** Real-world evidence for “agrivoltaics,” one of solarpunk’s favorite images: farms and solar panels helping each other.
