Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
More than five years after their formal recognition under the Clean Energy Package, energy communities remain marginal across the European Union. Drawing on participatory workshops and expert surveys conducted in 2021 and 2022 in six European countries within the Horizon 2020 EC 2 project, this paper argues that the persistent gap between policy ambition and empirical reality is not an implementation failure but a structural symptom of institutional lock-in within a neoliberal regime of energy governance analysed through the lens of institutional economics and commons theory. The barriers facing energy communities - regulatory, financial, infrastructural, and participatory - converge across countries with radically different regulatory traditions, indicating a structural rather than a discrete policy failure; where outcomes persist despite instruments enacted to change them, we read this as path dependency. The paper's principal contribution is to reframe the central question of the field: from “why do energy communities fail?” to “what must the institutional environment provide for them to emerge?” We set out nine meta-institutional conditions, organised in three layers, which spell out what Ostrom's design principles presuppose, rather than state, about the surrounding institutional environment.
