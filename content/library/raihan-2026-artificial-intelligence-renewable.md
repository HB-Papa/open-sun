Abstract from OpenAlex (for reference; not shown on the site):
The growing global demand for clean, reliable and low‑carbon electricity is driving a rapid transformation towards renewable‑dominated energy systems. This scoping review examines how artificial intelligence (AI) is being applied across the renewable energy value chain, with a focus on solar, wind, hydropower and hydrogen, as well as energy storage and smart grids. Following the PRISMA‑ScR framework, we systematically searched Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect and Web of Science for peer‑reviewed studies published over the last 15 years that apply machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning or other AI techniques to energy generation, forecasting, grid operation, storage management and decarbonisation strategies. The included literature shows that AI models consistently outperform classical baselines for renewable power forecasting, hybrid renewable energy system optimisation, and predictive maintenance of critical assets, often yielding substantial improvements in accuracy, efficiency and operational flexibility. At the same time, deployment at scale is constrained by data quality and availability, computational cost, model interpretability, cybersecurity concerns and gaps in regulatory and market design. Emerging work on explainable AI and physics‑informed learning offers promising avenues to increase transparency, robustness and trust in high‑stakes energy applications. Drawing together results across technologies and tasks, the review identifies priority directions for future research, including interoperable data infrastructures, scalable and energy‑efficient AI architectures, integrated uncertainty quantification and explainability, and socio‑technical studies on governance and energy justice. These findings highlight AI’s potential to enable more intelligent, resilient and equitable renewable‑dominated energy systems, while underscoring the need for coordinated advances in technology, policy and practice.
