## Start here

“Open access” means research that anyone can read online for free, with no login and no paywall. A great deal exists, but it is scattered across many websites. This guide shows you where to search, how to tell whether something is free to read, and what a Creative Commons license lets you do. You don’t need a university account for any of it.

## Where to search

| Tool | What it covers | One tip |
|---|---|---|
| [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) | A free, open catalog of research from every field | Turn on the open access filter to hide paywalled results. |
| [DOAJ](https://doaj.org) | The Directory of Open Access Journals. Journals apply and are checked before they are listed | Search for journals or for articles. |
| [CORE](https://core.ac.uk) | Open access papers gathered from university repositories and journals around the world | Good for finding the free copy of a paper you already know by title. |
| [BASE](https://www.base-search.net) | A search engine run by Bielefeld University Library that covers repositories worldwide | Not everything it lists is free to read, so check each result. |
| [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com) | Research of all kinds, free and paywalled together | Look for a [PDF] link to the right of a result, or click “All versions” underneath it. |
| [DOAB](https://www.doabooks.org) | The Directory of Open Access Books, for peer-reviewed academic books | Search by subject as well as by title. |
| [Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org) | A free browser extension | When you land on a paywalled article, it tells you if a legal free copy exists. |

## Search words that work

Start with one or two words, then narrow down.

Put quotation marks around words that belong together. This asks for the exact phrase. It works in OpenAlex and Google Scholar.

```
"energy justice"
```

In OpenAlex, you can join ideas with AND, OR and NOT. They must be in capital letters. Words with nothing between them are treated as AND.

```
"community solar" AND renters
```

```
degrowth NOT agriculture
```

In Google Scholar, OR works the same way. To leave a word out, put a minus sign straight in front of it.

```
"community solar" -perovskite
```

Too many results? Add a word. Too few? Remove one or try a synonym.

## How to tell if a source is trustworthy

Ask a few plain questions.

- **Who published it?** A university press, a research journal or a known organization is a good sign.
- **Was it peer-reviewed?** Peer review means other experts checked the work before it was published. Journal articles usually are. Blog posts and manifestos are not.
- **Is it a preprint?** A preprint is a paper shared before peer review. It can be useful and up to date, but its findings may still change.
- **Is the journal listed in DOAJ?** If it is, it has passed DOAJ’s checks.
- **Does it show its evidence?** Look for references and data.

## How to check you can read and reuse it

Reading and reusing are two different things.

**Reading.** You may find more than one version of the same paper. The published version is the final one on the journal’s site. An author manuscript is the author’s own copy of the accepted paper, shared legally in a repository. The layout and page numbers may differ.

**Reusing.** Look for a license on the first page or at the foot of the web page. Many open access works use a [Creative Commons license](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/). The letters tell you the rules.

| Letters | What they mean |
|---|---|
| BY | Give credit to the author. Every Creative Commons license below includes this. |
| SA | “ShareAlike.” If you adapt the work, share your version under the same license. |
| NC | “NonCommercial.” Don’t use it to make money. |
| ND | “NoDerivatives.” Share it as it is. Don’t share changed or translated versions. |

So CC BY-NC-ND means you may share the work with credit, not for profit, and unchanged.

If a work is free to read but shows no license, assume the usual copyright rules apply. Read it, cite it and link to it, but don’t repost the whole thing.

## Citing what you find

Every item page in this library has a “Cite this” box with a ready-made citation in APA and Chicago style.

For sources you find elsewhere, note the author, title, year, where it was published and the link. If the work has a DOI, a permanent identifier that starts with “10.”, save that too.
