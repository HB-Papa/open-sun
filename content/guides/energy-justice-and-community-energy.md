## Start here

Energy justice asks who benefits from an energy system, who carries its burdens, and who gets a say. Community energy is one practical answer: solar, wind or storage that local people own or share.

If these ideas are new to you, read the Field Guide entries for [energy justice](/field-guide#energy-justice), [energy democracy](/field-guide#energy-democracy) and [community solar](/field-guide#community-solar) first. Then come back here to search further.

## Where to search

| Tool | What it covers | One tip |
|---|---|---|
| [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) | A free, open catalog of research from every field | Turn on the open access filter, then sort by date to see the newest work. |
| [DOAJ](https://doaj.org) | Journals and articles that are fully open access | Good for finding whole journals on energy and society. |
| [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com) | Research of all kinds, free and paywalled together | Click “Cited by” under a key paper to find newer work that builds on it. |
| [OSTI.GOV](https://www.osti.gov) | Research funded by the U.S. Department of Energy, including national laboratory reports | Strong on numbers: costs, capacity and who has access. |
| [Berkeley Lab Energy Markets and Policy](https://emp.lbl.gov) | Free reports and data on solar adoption, costs and fairness in the United States | Many reports come with a short summary or slides. |
| [IRENA publications](https://www.irena.org/publications) | Free reports from the International Renewable Energy Agency | Useful for a view beyond one country. |

## Search words that work

Researchers use different words for nearly the same idea. If one word finds little, try its neighbors.

| Idea | Words to try |
|---|---|
| Fairness in energy | "energy justice", "just transition", "energy poverty", "energy sovereignty" |
| People having a say | "energy democracy", "energy commons", participation |
| Shared local projects | "community solar", "community energy", "energy community", "community-owned" |
| How the public responds | "social acceptance", "public support", "public opposition" |
| Farming under solar panels | agrivoltaics, agrivoltaic |
| Harm from clean energy projects | "sacrifice zones", "green extractivism" |

Put quotation marks around a phrase to search for those exact words together.

```
"energy justice"
```

In OpenAlex, join ideas with AND, OR and NOT in capital letters.

```
"community solar" AND "low-income"
```

```
"energy democracy" OR "energy commons"
```

**Leave out the engineering.** Searches for solar topics pull in many engineering papers about panel materials and electronics. If that is not what you want, leave those words out. In OpenAlex, use NOT.

```
"community energy" NOT perovskite
```

Spellings and terms vary by country. “Energy community” is common in European research, and “community solar” in the United States. Try both.

## How to tell if a source is trustworthy

Energy has strong interests on every side, so ask a few questions.

- **Who paid for it?** Reports from utilities, industry groups and campaign groups can be accurate and still be selective. Look for a funding or disclosure statement.
- **Is it peer-reviewed research, a government report or an advocacy piece?** All three can be useful. Say which one it is when you cite it.
- **Where and when?** A finding about one country, state or year may not hold elsewhere. Prices and policies change fast.
- **Does it show its data?** Good work says where its numbers come from.
- **Is it a preprint?** A preprint has not yet been through peer review. Treat its findings as early.

## How to check you can read and reuse it

Many energy papers sit behind paywalls, but a legal free copy often exists. Look for an “author manuscript” in a university or laboratory repository. It is the author’s own copy of the accepted paper. Google Scholar’s “All versions” link often leads to one.

Works by the U.S. federal government are generally free of copyright within the United States. Reports from other bodies carry their own terms, so look for a license or a “terms of use” link before you reuse charts or long passages.

## Citing what you find

Every item page in this library has a “Cite this” box with APA and Chicago citations you can copy.

For reports, note the organization as well as any named authors. For a preprint or author manuscript, say so in your citation.
