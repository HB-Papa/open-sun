## Overview

This lesson is for students starting a research project who type a whole question into a search box and take whatever comes back. It works with one question: does community solar reach renters and lower-income households?

Energy justice suits the frame Searching as Strategic Exploration. Researchers use many words for nearly the same idea, and solar searches pull in engineering papers a social question doesn’t need. Students meet both problems fast, and learn that a search is a series of choices to adjust, not a single try.

## Before class

- Run every search string below at [openalex.org](https://openalex.org) and [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com) the day before. OpenAlex’s help pages document capital AND, OR and NOT, quotation marks and parentheses, but they don’t say outright that the basic search box handles all of them. Note anything that behaves differently.
- Save screenshots of your results in case the network fails.
- Share the handouts: [Energy Justice and Community Energy](/guides/energy-justice-and-community-energy) and [Finding Open Access Research](/guides/finding-open-access-research).
- Open [Energy Justice: A Conceptual Review](/library/jenkins-2016-energy-justice-review) and the three records in step 10 in tabs.
- Prepare a shared document or paper copies of the search log in step 2.

## Lesson plan

| Time | Activity | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | First try | Type the question into OpenAlex as written and judge the first five results. |
| 5–13 | Concepts and words | In pairs, split the question into concepts and add terms from the energy guide. |
| 13–23 | Build and revise | Write a search, run it, log the count, change one thing, run it again. |
| 23–33 | Follow the trail | In Google Scholar, open “Cited by” under a key paper and narrow to recent years. |
| 33–43 | Free copy and version | Find a free copy of one result and decide which version it is. |
| 43–50 | Share and exit | Post your best search and one change you made, with your reason. |

## The activity

1. **Start with the question.** Does community solar reach renters and lower-income households? Type it into OpenAlex as a full sentence. Note the number of results and whether the first five answer it.
2. **Make a search log** with four columns: search string, number of results, what I noticed, what I’ll change next.
3. **Find the concepts.** Underline the two or three ideas that matter, such as community solar, renters and lower income. Leave out words like “does” and “reach.”
4. **Add words.** Use the table in [Energy Justice and Community Energy](/guides/energy-justice-and-community-energy) to list at least two terms per concept, such as “community energy,” tenants or “energy poverty.”
5. **Build a search in OpenAlex.** Put phrases in quotation marks. Join ideas with AND, OR and NOT in capital letters. Use parentheses to keep OR groups together.

   ```
   "community solar" AND renters
   ```

   ```
   ("community solar" OR "community energy") AND ("low-income" OR renters OR tenants)
   ```

6. **Revise on purpose.** Too many results? Add a concept. Too few? Add an OR term or drop a concept. Engineering papers crowding in? Leave them out with NOT.

   ```
   "community energy" NOT perovskite
   ```

7. **Narrow to free work** with the open access filter beside your results. Log the new count.
8. **Follow a key paper forward.** In Google Scholar, search for a title in quotation marks.

   ```
   "Energy justice: a conceptual review"
   ```

   Click “Cited by” to see newer papers that cite it. Use “Since Year” on the left to keep only recent work.
9. **Find a free copy.** Look for a [PDF] link to the right of a result, or click “All versions.” Check the first page: is it the published version, an author manuscript or a preprint?
10. **Compare with the library.** Three records show the three versions. [Evaluating Community Solar](/library/oshaughnessy-2024-community-solar-equitable-access) links to the authors’ preprint, [Conceptualizing Energy Democracy](/library/szulecki-2018-energy-democracy) to an author manuscript, and [Reconciliation through Renewable Energy?](/library/hoicka-2021-reconciliation-renewable-energy) to the published article. Where you see “Publisher’s page (DOI),” click it to find the final version.
11. **Post your best search** and one sentence on a change you made and why.

## Assessment

Read the search logs as pairs work and the posted strings at the end. Steps 3 and 4 show the first outcome, steps 5 to 7 the second, and step 9 the third.

| Outcome | Beginning | Developing | Proficient |
|---|---|---|---|
| Concepts and terms | Searches the whole sentence | Names concepts, one term each | Names concepts, two or more terms each |
| Building and revising | One search, unchanged | Uses quotation marks or AND, logs no reasons | Uses quotation marks and capital operators, logs a reason for each change |
| Free copy and version | Finds a page but not the full text | Finds a free copy, can’t name its version | Finds a free copy and names its version with evidence |

## Adapting this lesson

- **Online or asynchronous:** Use a form for the log. Students post before-and-after strings and suggest one synonym to a classmate.
- **Shorter session (30 minutes):** Keep steps 1 to 7 and set steps 8 to 10 as homework.
- **Captions:** Caption any recorded demonstration, and read search strings aloud including the quotation marks.
- **Screen readers:** Give search strings as copyable text, never only as screenshots.
- **Low vision:** Allow browser zoom and check that screenshots stay legible enlarged.
- **Students on phones:** Every tool runs in a phone browser, but menus may sit in different places. Pair phone and laptop users for steps 7 to 9, and give links students can tap.
- **Multilingual learners:** Logs can be in any language. Point students to the [Field Guide](/field-guide) for key terms, and invite them to try a term from another language.

## Notes for the instructor

- **“I got zero results.”** Usually too many ANDs or a long phrase. Remove one concept and try again.
- **“Why capitals?”** OpenAlex’s help pages say AND, OR and NOT must be uppercase to work as operators.
- **“Do I need ‘renter’ and ‘renters’?”** No. OpenAlex matches word endings, so “renters” finds “renter.” Quoted phrases are matched as written.
- **“The minus sign didn’t work.”** OpenAlex’s help pages say a minus sign doesn’t exclude words there. Use NOT.
- **“Isn’t the preprint good enough?”** Often, for reading. Knowing the version lets students check the final one before quoting numbers.
- **The open access filter is approximate.** It relies on OpenAlex’s data, which can be wrong. Students should open each source to confirm it is free.
